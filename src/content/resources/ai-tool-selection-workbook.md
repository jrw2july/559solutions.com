## What is inside

The free guide on this site gives you the five steps and eight questions. This workbook is the working version: the tables you fill in, in the order you use them, with one complete example to copy from.

1. **Define the work.** One recurring task, who does it, what starts it, what it produces, and three real cases timed instead of estimated.
2. **Choose the category, not the brand.** A table of tool categories by the job they do, and the two or three candidates to compare.
3. **Fit check.** The people who will use it, where it sits in the workflow, and whether it works inside the systems you already run.
4. **Data and risk.** Three data classes (Public, Internal, Restricted), what the work touches, and the questions to answer before any restricted information goes near a tool.
5. **The scorecard.** Eight weighted criteria with written meanings for every score. The Excel version totals itself.
6. **The pilot playbook.** A short, repeatable process another person could follow: trigger, input, steps, review, output, measure and stop rule.
7. **The run log.** One row per run, and how to read the pattern after ten to twenty.
8. **The decision record.** Keep, adjust or stop, with the reasons and a trigger for looking again.

The worked example follows Prairie Bookkeeping, a made-up four-person firm, through all eight worksheets, including a pilot that keeps client financial details out of the tool entirely.

## What you need to start

One piece of work in mind, a free trial account on each tool you compare, and a few hours spread over two or three weeks. No technical background is needed. It is tool-neutral and names no vendors; for current tool names, use the free quick reference in the [Code559 guide](/code559/choose-the-right-ai-tool/).
